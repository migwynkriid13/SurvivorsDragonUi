# Equipped jewelry

The user selected action-bar attachment design **4 — Thin shared bezel**, with the extra inner/lower curved line beneath the gold circles removed. The original bar artwork is retained, adding only five upper socket extensions. There is no jewelry panel, permanent title or side ornament. The row uses the character's five normal equipment slots: necklace, first ring, second ring, first trinket and second trinket. It is independent of the native six-pickup run build and does not add an ability row or infer run jewelry from worn gear, stats or chat.

## Data and interaction

`modules/ui/jewelry.lua` resolves `NeckSlot`, `Finger0Slot`, `Finger1Slot`, `Trinket0Slot` and `Trinket1Slot` through `GetInventorySlotInfo`, requiring five distinct valid native IDs. `GetInventoryItemTexture("player", id)` supplies each current icon. Item art uses a centered texture crop with exactly 5% added magnification in both attached and detached modes; circle and rim dimensions are unchanged. An empty or unavailable texture leaves that socket blank. No sample icons, item IDs, item names, item statistics, invented ranks or sixth slot are shipped.

Hover shows only the equipped item's native **Equip**, **Use** and **Chance on hit** effect descriptions. A private `GameTooltipTemplate` under a permanently hidden parent reads `SetInventoryItem("player", id)`; the shared `GameTooltip` is never owned, cleared or restyled. The reader scans at most 64 native rows, skipping the item title and selecting effect rows by the native localized `ITEM_SPELL_TRIGGER_*` prefixes, with English fallback labels only when those globals are missing. The complete matched trigger label and its following whitespace are removed for display, including multiword labels such as Chance on hit. Effect wording, numbers and wrapped text are preserved; no arbitrary first word of a description is removed. Binding, equipment type, requirements, flavor text, sale value and separate cooldown metadata are omitted. No effect values are inferred from the icon or item name. Missing APIs, unavailable descriptions or items without recognized effect rows produce no popup; a hovered slot retries at the existing five-Hz cadence so late native data can appear.

Twelve user-requested English effect sentences use compact display wording after trigger-label removal:

- `Increases your movement speed by X%` becomes `+ Movement speed: X%`.
- `Reduces the cooldown of your spells by X%` becomes `- X% Cooldown`.
- `Reduces all damage taken by X%` becomes `- X% Damage taken`.
- `Increases your maximum health by X%` becomes `+ Maximum health: X%`.
- `Improves your chance to get a critical strike by X%` becomes `+ Critical strike chance: X%`.
- `Increases the damage of your critical strikes by X%` becomes `+ Critical damage: X%`.
- `Increases the range you pick up loot from by X%` becomes `+ Loot pickup range: X%`.
- `Increases gold found by X%` becomes `+ Gold found: X%`.
- `Increases the area of effect of your spells by X%` becomes `+ Spell area: X%`.
- `Increases all damage you deal by X%` becomes `+ Damage dealt: X%`.
- `Your damage, haste, shield and thorns buffs are X% stronger` becomes `+ Buff power: X%`.
- `Restores X health per second` becomes `+ Health regen: X/s`.

The number is copied from the native description, preserving integer or decimal text without rounding or a fixed item value. Percentage effects retain `%`; regeneration retains health per second through `/s`. Buff power abbreviates the native damage, haste, shield and thorns buff-strength sentence. Matching tolerates wrapped whitespace and an optional final period, and requires the complete sentence so compound effects are not shortened accidentally. Other descriptions and languages retain their native wording after trigger-label removal. These rewrites affect only jewelry hover text, with no change to equipment-slot selection, gameplay stats, other item tooltips or chat formatting. Armor and flat Health lines in the supplied inventory examples remain item metadata outside the effects-only card.

The effects appear above the hovered circle in a compact, mouse-transparent dark card with a bronze diamond-metal border, subtle gold divider and green DEFAULT 13 text. The panel reuses the shipped Dragonflight-family artwork and helpers from `core/editor.lua`, with smaller 12-unit border cells and no header. Its background starts three units inside each edge, overlapping beneath the smaller border's inner bevel so the fill joins the metal without a transparent gap. Normal editor panels retain their existing fill inset. It wraps within a 272-unit text column, measures height through the native 1.12 `GetStringWidth`/`GetHeight` methods and requests screen clamping when available. It works in attached and detached modes and clears on mouse leave, owner hiding, equipment removal, editing, disable and world leave/logout. The panel and scanner are pooled; no hover or effect state is saved. Artwork provenance is in [SDUI-ARTWORK.md](../third_party/SDUI-ARTWORK.md).

The base client's [item-tooltip renderer](https://github.com/samwhosung/benilla/blob/f55bb79b1343629df2705d9e4207a7612a886253/crates/benilla-ui/src/script/tooltip_item/render.rs#L615-L627) emits these trigger descriptions as distinct wrapped rows. This supports filtering the native description without reimplementing item calculations; it does not establish packaged-game behavior or expose a new jewelry API.

The row has no item-use, equip, pickup, replacement or drag handlers outside shared layout editing. Optional cooldown swipes read only native `GetInventoryItemCooldown` start/duration/enable values and use `GetTime` on the same UI clock. A generated 64-state circular shade atlas visualizes the remaining fraction without putting a square model or dark corner cover around the icon. Missing optional APIs omit those features; they do not invent gameplay timing or disable valid icons.

The base client's [inventory readers](https://github.com/samwhosung/benilla/blob/f55bb79b1343629df2705d9e4207a7612a886253/crates/benilla-ui/src/script/char_stats.rs) establish the ordinary equipped-item API and native cooldown clock, separate from the supplied AZS run tables. Source presence does not confirm the exact packaged client's live equipment publication or rendering.

Refresh is bounded to five reads per second per slot while in the world, with world-entry refresh and inventory/cooldown events scheduling the next refresh. Polling also allows native template data that arrives after an inventory event to appear. Cosmetic shade painting is bounded to 30 Hz while visible and changes UVs only when the atlas state changes. Missing required APIs or no native player hides the row and clears icons, cooldowns and owned tooltips. World leave/logout clear them immediately and stop reads/painting until world entry. No equipment or cooldown data is saved.

## Layout and original build

By default, the five gold circles line up with action buttons **2 through 6**, one above each button. Center spacing matches button width plus its six-unit gap. While attached, the row is parented to the action bar, so its attachment follows the bar's confirmed position, uniform scale, visibility and editing draft. At the 36-unit reference button size, each visible gold circle is about 29 units wide, its outer socket is 40 units wide, and its center is 20 units above the button tops. The rim crop removes uneven transparent padding while preserving the visible size; each rim shares its icon's center anchor. The socket rail is anchored directly to the action bar, with its centers at the same positions.

`SocketRail.tga` combines all five socket arches, feet and straight spans in one continuous upper-edge texture, prepared from the existing `HDActionBar.tga`. It replaces the previous separate caps and gap pieces, which could place the feet and straight edge on different screen rows. The rail uses one bar-owned rectangle and one UV mapping; socket feet have no independent vertical anchors or scale. The original bar texture file is unchanged. Only its 12-unit upper strip switches to the rail; gaps retain the original native pixels and the lower background retains its original UV mapping. The rail uses the native bevel's color, alpha and vertical alignment at the join. Original dark bar material sits inside the border and below the bends; circular openings stay transparent. Transparent cutouts prevent the old straight edge from crossing empty socket openings. No inner/lower quarter-circle line is drawn. When jewelry is unavailable or hidden, the original complete bar background is restored.

The original 317-by-88 internal jewelry frame remains. Attached mode uses **Action bar** for the combined assembly, extending its edit region to include the sockets. Detached mode parents the same icons to a separate UIParent-owned container registered as **Equipped jewelry (detached)**, with its own uniform scale, location and optional hidden flag under the existing `jewelryBar` layout key. This keeps attached and detached geometry separate without moving either registered owner between parents. Confirm, Cancel, Hide frame, discard and full reset use the shared lifecycle. Earlier standalone geometry is reused only when detached; it never moves the attached row. Reattaching retains detached geometry for the next detachment. No migration flag or SavedVariable is added. Existing action-bar geometry, other groups' geometry, native button handlers and equipment-data behavior are preserved. Both layout groups suppress jewelry tooltips while editing.

**Equipped jewelry** in `/sdui` → **HUD** defaults on. Turning it off hides the five icons, rims and socket extensions, restores the original complete action-bar edge and contracts the action-bar editing bounds. Equipment reads and cooldown painting stop while disabled. The toggle previews immediately, saves its normalized `equippedJewelry` boolean in the existing `SurvivorsDragonUiDisplay` table only with Confirm, and rolls back on Cancel, world leave/logout/reload. Both full resets restore on. It does not change native build visibility or action-bar geometry.

**Attach jewelry to action bar** defaults on and is dimmed and mouse-disabled while Equipped jewelry is off. It retains its value while disabled. Off replaces the attached rail with five complete silver-gray circular outlines from `SocketDetached.tga` and restores the original action-bar edge. Independent defaults derive from the static bar appearance metadata: the detached circles' bottom sits four units above the default action-art canvas top, without reading moved/scaled bar geometry. Only Confirm saves the normalized `attachJewelry` boolean in the existing display table. Cancel and world leave/logout/reload discard mode and detached-layout drafts; both full resets restore attached mode and default geometry. The native-build visibility choice remains independent.

The independent **Hide default jewelry / abilities** HUD toggle defaults on. `Native build rows` in `modules/ui/ui.lua` feature-detects the official getter/setter and applies the inverse of the `hideNativeBuild` display boolean on initialization, world entry, display changes and native `hudBuild` changes while in the world. Turning it off explicitly shows both original weapon/jewelry rows. Matching visibility causes no repeated write, and synchronous callbacks are guarded. Confirm alone saves the boolean in the existing display table. Cancel and world leave/logout/reload restore the committed choice; the module discards display drafts before world-exit or native reload saving, independently of event-handler order. Both full resets restore hiding. There is no per-frame polling, prior-visibility snapshot or new SavedVariable. Clock, encounter and tally takeover are unchanged. Hiding the native six-pickup build does not establish a data mapping between those pickups and the five equipped items.

Artwork sources, circular clipping and conversion are recorded in [Jewelry-ARTWORK.md](../third_party/Jewelry-ARTWORK.md). GitHub Actions checks formatting, local dead code and TOC version metadata; in-game behavior remains unverified.
